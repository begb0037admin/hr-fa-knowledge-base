What is the context of this article?

A Debtor Override is a rule used in billing systems when the organisation receiving the invoice is different from the organisation that owns the charge band or originally requested the service.

The Debtor Override rule in Meddbase allows you to invoice selected services to a third party, which is different from the company responsible for the charge band. This allows the service activity to still be recorded against the charge band, but the invoice is redirected to another named company (the override debtor). This ensures that specific services are billed correctly when a third party, partner organisation, sponsor, or funder is responsible for payment rather than the charge band owner.

For example, the service belongs to Company A, but the invoice needs to go to Company B (a third party). The Debtor Override tells the system to bill Company B instead.

Why is a Debtor Override Needed? 

Debtor Overrides are used to ensure the correct customer is billed, especially when:



    

  • A service is provided to one company but paid for by another.

    • 

  • A third-party sponsor or partner is responsible for the charges.

    • 

  • A customer requests centralised billing or wants invoices directed to a parent or affiliated organisation.

    • 

  • Costs must go to an external funding organisation rather than the operational unit.
    • 




  
    What are the pre-requisites for the the debtor override rule?
  





Before applying a Debtor Override, the following must be configured:


  • The Charge Band must still have a valid Provide Services rule assigned.
  • A Price List must be linked to the Charge Band so that invoices can be generated.
  • The system still needs a price on the originating Charge Band, even when the invoice is redirected.
  • The Debtor Override payer company must be a valid debtor in the system.
Once these conditions are met, the Debtor Override rule can successfully transfer charges to the designated company.
The Debtor Override rule cannot be applied to the following: 


  • A Self‑Pay or Company Only Charge Band
  • The internal company
  • The same company as the debtor company (i.e., you cannot override a debtor to itself)

  Scenario: Redirect invoices 






A patient is admitted under a Charge Band owned by Company A (responsible for the admission and general care). Per a commercial agreement, specific services (e.g., MRI Scan, Consultant Review, Diagnostic Tests) must be invoiced to Company B instead of Company A. 
Why use the Debtor Override Rule?
To redirect invoices for selected services to Company B while keeping the service activity recorded against Company A’s Charge Band. This prevents manual rework, misdirected invoices, and contract non‑compliance.
What Changes for Users (Booking & Visibility)
  • Service Picker
  • For services covered by the Debtor Override Rule, the Payer column shows Company B (the override debtor).
  • All other services show Company A (the Charge Band owner).
  • Payer Type (during booking)
  • When an overridden service is selected, the Payer Type displays Company B, not the Charge Band’s payer.
  • No manual payer changes are required
  • The Debtor Override Rule applies automatically to the services it controls.
