Custom Reports

If ReadySet's standard reports don't quite provide the information you need, you can use the Custom Report feature to generate an ad hoc report of specific data points in a survey, charting form, and demographics, choose from specific fields in those selections and/or limit certain parameters, and export the results to a spreadsheet for further evaluation.

You can pick specific questions in a survey and/or charting form, along with specific dates or demographics, to filter the data to report on, and then choose what columns you want to see in the output.

Examples

  • Find out how many employees have completed their TB Symptom Survey and who has not, to identify their compliance rate. Identify anyone who has answered "Yes" to symptoms in order to follow up and assess for true exposure.
  • Get a total count of healthcare workers that care for the pediatric population who have received a Tdap vaccine and identify those due for a booster.
  • Identify employees who have completed a Self-Reported Incident Survey, when the incident occurred, and if anyone sought medical treatment, in order to follow up with Workers' Comp.

To create a custom report:

  1. Go to Reports > Custom Reports.
  2. On the left side, define the criteria filters: Demographics, Charting Form, and Survey.
  3. Select from any combination of these filters to narrow down your search criteria.
    Note: These are filter criteria only, to limit your search — you will choose the actual fields to display in the report later.
    • In the Demographics section, click + and select the Demographic field that you want to search on, then click Apply search criteria to add the selected field to the Demographics section.
      Click + again to add more fields as required.
       
    • In the Charting Form section, select the chart from the dropdown field or being typing the name to find it, then click + to select from that chart's questions that you want to search on.
    • In the Survey section, select the survey and specific questions you want to search on.
      Note: When you select a question in either of these two sections, you also select the response you want to include (or select the Not checkbox to exclude).
    • Avoid choosing questions that require a text response (e.g. "If other reason, please specify") since the text must match exactly to what's in the record. Dates and yes/no questions yield the best results.
    • Once selected, the question is listed in the appropriate filter section (Charting Form or Survey) where you can edit or remove it, or click + to add another question.
    • Questions added to the filter section as shown above are treated as "AND" conditions; a record must match all the questions/responses to be included in the search results.
      If you want results that meet at least one but not necessarily all criteria, you can define "OR" questions:  when adding a question click Add OR and select the other question/response. For example, if you want to see the latest Pfizer Booster dose received, you can choose COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2) Immunization Record and in the selection filter criteria, "Latest vaccine received: Pfizer Bivalent" Add OR "Pfizer-Biontech" and it will give you both or one of your choices. In this case, you want results that meet at least one of the defined criteria.

    • Optionally, select the Last completed results only checkbox to limit the results to the most recent results; if this is not selected, you will get all historical results.
    • For any filter criteria that is a date, for example order date or completion date, you can filter by Before, After, Between, Equals, or Last to find matches. 
  4. Once you have defined all your search criteria, click Search at the bottom of the Filter Criteria panel. 
    If the query is too big, you will either receive an error message or you will receive no results at all. You will need to choose different criteria or modify existing criteria when using more than one filter section. The Search Results section on the right side of the page displays all the records that match the filter criteria.
  5. Click Select Columns to further refine output results to display more meaningful information.
    You may choose to hide columns that are not important (e.g. some demographic fields), and add columns that may be more useful. For example, while the results only show participants who answered "Yes" to the question "Have ever been told by a healthcare provider that you had a positive TB Test?", you may also want to see how they responded to other questions, e.g.  specific TB symptoms, or "When was your last TB skin test?". Click     Apply Selected to refresh the results. This will not change the number of records shown, only which fields are displayed for each. The number of records shown are dictated by the initial filter criteria.

    On reviewing the results, you may want to filter the results differently. For example, there were two participants who responded "Yes" to some of the symptoms, so you want more information to determine if follow-up is required. Or there may be a large number of results over several pages that you want to filter down further.

  6. You can either add more columns, or go back to filter the data further to limit the results more precisely. In our example, we added a charting form filter to only include participants who have been seen by a nurse for a symptom review before a specific date, and then added columns to view the next due date.
    Alternatively, you can export the results to CSV to examine and manipulate in a spreadsheet. The resulting file will be found on the Download Reports tab.
