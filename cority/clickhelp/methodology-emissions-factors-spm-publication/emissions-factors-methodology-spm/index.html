Methodology - Emissions Factors - SPM

The Cority Sustainability software solutions uses the GHG Protocol methodology for calculating the global emissions of our clients. Under this methodology, we use nationally recognised emissions factors, published by regulated authorities to ensure the robustness of our calculations. Emissions factors are applied at a datasource level in our solutions to ensure clients are using the most appropriate sets.

The emissions factors database includes over 1 million time-stamped, gas-specific emissions factors from sources including: GHG Protocol, EPA (USA), NGA (Australia), Defra (UK), CGGI (Canada), CEA (India), SEAI (Ireland), Spanish Government (Spain), Bilan Carbone (France) and IEA (International). The software also provides the ability for clients to input their own supplier specific emissions factors to meet the requirements of Scope 2 GHG Protocol reporting and apply US Residual Mix and RE-DISS II Residual Mix factors aligned with this methodology.

Location-Based Emissions Factors

Emissions factors in the database are assembled from a variety of sources, each with their own unique requirements. The following sources would be possible for clients to select from:

Central Electricity Authority (CEA)

The Central Electricity Authority of India first published emissions factors for electricity in 2007 and these are updated regularly.

Canada Greenhouse Gas Intensity (CGGI)

These emissions factors are found in the Canadian National Inventory Report which is updated annually. These emissions factors are for most fuels, electricity and waste to landfill.

Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra)

This methodology was first published by the authorities in the United Kingdom in 2005 and except for 2006 has been updated on an annual basis. For methodologies published up to and including 2012, the emissions factors provided in a particular year applied for two years in arrears i.e. the 2012 factors applied from 2010. As the emissions factors were updated, the emissions from the previous two years could be re-calculated if preferred but this was at the client’s discretion.

Up to 2012, the grid electricity emissions factors were calculated using a five-year rolling average and thus these were generally updated annually for the previous five years plus an additional year for the methodology being added. It was usually recommended that electricity emissions should be re-calculated for all applicable years but this was at the client’s discretion.

Since 2013 the emissions factors published apply for the year of publication e.g. Defra 2020 applies from January-December 2020. Defra’s annual factor updates are usually published in the summer of each year meaning that the Cority client services team engages with clients on if they would like to reprocess mid-year to apply new factors. Additionally, in 2013, all electricity emissions factors were re-stated to be for a single year.

Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)

Emissions factors for the United States are published by the EPA, apply to most datasources and are updated on a regular basis. The EPA publish two sets of electricity factors for the United States i.e. for the actual states and for the e-grid regions. Both are implemented in Cority Sustainability.

France - Bilan Carbone

This methodology was published by the French government in 2007 and has not been updated in Sustainability Performance Management. To provide a Bilan Carbone carbon emissions report the consultant needs to be verified by the French authorities.

GHG Protocol

GHG Protocol provides both the overarching methodology for calculating GHG emissions and some specific emissions factors that can be applied. This protocol was first published in 2001 under the auspices of the World Resources Institute (WRI) and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD). The emissions factors are updated on an ad hoc basis and are drawn from several sources e.g. Defra in the United Kingdom, Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) in the United States and the International Energy Agency (IEA) for country specific electricity emissions factors.

International Energy Agency (IEA) - electricity only

Since 2012, the country specific electricity emissions factors have been sourced directly from the IEA and these are updated annually by Cority. As these factors apply to 2 years in arrears (i.e. IEA 2020 applies for year 2018) it is recommended that all applicable electricity data files are re-processed to use the latest emissions factors but this is only a recommendation and not mandatory so is done at the clients discretion. The IEA factors cover global energy at a country level. Cority pays an annual software licence for IEA factors to be included in our solutions. Under this licence, emissions factors can be accessed by clients from within the software (e.g. Data Audit dashboard) but clients are not allowed to bulk access replicable, sharable or raw documents associated with the factors.

National Greenhouse Accounts (NGA)

This methodology is published by the Australian government and emissions factors are updated annually.

Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland (SEAI)

This methodology is published by the Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland and the electricity emissions factors are updated annually. Several fuel emissions factors were originally published but these have not been updated by the SEAI since.

Spain

This methodology is published by the Spanish government and the Catalonian Climate Office and emissions factors are updated by them annually. Some year’s additional emissions factors have been provided and these are then added to Enterprise.

Market-Based Emissions Factors

Association of Issuing Bodies - European Residual Mixes

Prior to 2015 the residual mix factors for Europe were published by Reliable Disclosure Systems for Europe – Phase II. Since 2015 these factors have been published by the Association of Issuing Bodies - European Residual Mixes. These factors are updated annually.

Green-e Energy Residual Mix Emissions Rates

The Green-e factors are published by the EPA annually for the United States e-grid regions. From 2015 to 2018 they included factors for the Canadian regions as well.

Checking for Emissions Factor Updates

The Sustainability Product Development Manager checks the sources of current emissions factors on a quarterly basis to determine if there have been any updates to emissions factors. If there have been updates to these factors, these are added to the emissions factor specifications for a subsequent release.

Updating Emissions Factors in the Database

When updated or new emissions factors are published, the Product Team transfer these to the relevant specifications and set the dates that these apply to. If there are updates to current emissions factors, the last set of emissions factors will have their applicable dates adjusted to reflect this. This means that when the Defra 2020 methodology was published the Defra 2019 emissions factors had their ‘To’ date changed to 31 December 2019 instead of the current ‘To’ date of ‘Present’. The emissions factors from Defra 2020 then apply for the ‘From’ date of 1 January 2020 to the ‘To’ date of ‘Present’.

For electricity, the emissions factors for several years might change depending on how many years have been updated in the new methodology. For example, when new IEA factors were published in September 2020 these applied from 1 January 2018 onwards. Thus the 2017 factors that applied from 1 January 2017 to Present had their ‘To’ date changed to 31 December 2017 and the new factors apply from 1 January 2018 to ‘Present’.

Once the updated specifications have been signed off by the Head of Client Services these are added to source control which is the specification repository access by the Cority Sustainability solutions.

Once the emissions factors have been updated in the database, this is tested by the Product Development Manager who loads data to all the data sources that have had emissions factor updates for the applicable time periods. This testing ensures that the calculations are still accurate and that the applicable emissions factors for the given criteria including dates are being applied.

Once this testing has been completed, the updated emissions factor library is made available to the clients. At this stage if required client’s data should be re-processed to use these updated emissions factors.

The GHG Protocol recommends to clients that they use the latest emissions factors as this ensures that their total emissions are comparable to other companies.

Communicating Emissions Factor Updates to Clients

Where possible, emissions factor updates are applied as part of the standard two-monthly release cycle and when upgrades are scheduled all clients receive an email to inform them of the date and time of the upgrade. This email message contains a list of all the new / updated features for the upcoming release including if there are any emissions factor updates. On the day of the upgrade, a reminder email is sent out and once the upgrade is complete a third message is sent informing clients that the upgrade is complete. Both messages contain the list of new / updated features including any emissions factor updates.

If emissions factor updates are implemented as a standalone update and not part of a release, the Product Development Manager will send out an email to all clients informing them of this.

In all instance these email messages are sent to the main users at each client and not to all users at each client.

Scope 2 Market-Based and Company Derived Emission Factors

If clients would like to report using their own emissions factors (based on a set of company-specific assumptions or provided by suppliers) the functionality exists under Market-Based Reporting. This allows clients to capture these Scope 2 specific emissions, either as absolute carbon emissions (tCO2e) or using a custom input emissions factor.

As per the GHG Protocol Scope 2 (electricity) reporting requirements, it is advised that companies report both location-based and market-based as best practice.

The ability to capture absolute or calculate emissions using client specific emissions factors for all data sources has been implemented in Enterprise. This information needs to be included with all relevant data rows during the data upload process and will only apply to the client that has uploaded this data.

The location and market-based calculations can be carried out in parallel in the software, allowing clients to select which type of reporting they would like to do on all dashboards and reports (Location or Market-Based).

Market-Based Emissions

Cority enables dual reporting as recommended under the GHG Protocol. If a user selects Location-based analysis on a dashboard or report, only the location-based emissions that were calculated using the emissions factors library will be displayed. If Market-Based analysis is selected, the software will display GHG emissions based on the calculation priority process shown below:

Market-Based calculation prioritisation:

  1. Priority 1: Market-based CO2e input by a user
  2. Priority 2: Market-based CO2e factor multiplied by consumption input by a user
  3. Priority 3 (electricity only): Residual Mix emissions factors
  4. Priority 4: location-based emissions factors
