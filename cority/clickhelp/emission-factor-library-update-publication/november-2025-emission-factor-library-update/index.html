The new factors are not automatically applied to data that was previously uploaded to the system. Existing data files must be reprocessed to apply the updated emission factors. For support on managing this process or any other questions, please contact Cority Support.

NGA 2025 (Australia) 

The latest emission factors from the Australian Government, as published in National Greenhouse Account Factors 2025, are available with the new factors being applicable from July 1, 2025. No major methodological shifts have taken place, aside from the addition of a factor for gaseous hydrogen as a fuel source for stationary combustion. 

OCCC 2025 (Spain) 

Emission factors for OCCC (Spain) have been updated based on the GHG emissions calculator 2024 (2025 version), published in 2025. These factors apply from January 1, 2025. This methodology now applies IPCC AR6 global warming potentials, meaning adjustments from the previous year in terms of the impact of CH4 and N2O. Previously, this methodology used global warming potential from IPCC AR5. 

A minor adjustment has been made to CH4 and N2O for biodiesel and 100% mineral diesel/petrol (i.e., without any biofuel), where an average is now used across vehicle types. This is consistent with the method used for other fuel/vehicle types, where a single CH4 or N2O value is not provided by the methodology. Standard retail fuels are unaffected. In addition, there is a new rail freight category that includes a hybrid mode of electricity and diesel (FGC Hybrid Freight (electricity + diesel B)). No other major methodological changes have been noted. 

EPA 2025 (USA) – Addition of Waste by Volume 

New factors have been added to allow users to report based on the volume of waste produced, supplementing all existing mass-based factors provided in the EPA’s GHG Emission Factors Hub, which are developed from the EPA’s Waste Reduction Model (WARM). The conversions applied come from Volume-to-Weight Conversion Factors U.S. Environmental Protection Agency Office of Resource Conservation and Recovery, published in April 2016, as an update to the original volume-to-weight conversion factors published in 1997. Where ranges are provided, we have applied a median value.
