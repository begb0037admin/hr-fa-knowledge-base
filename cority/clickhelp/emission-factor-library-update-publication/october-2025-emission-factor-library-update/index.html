IEA Emission factors for Electricity 2025 

The new factors are not automatically applied to data that was previously uploaded to the system. Existing data files must be reprocessed to apply the updated emission factors. For support on managing this process or any other questions, please contact Cority Support.

The latest emission factors from IEA Emissions Factors 2025, published by the IEA, include new values for the 2023 reporting year. These are available in Cority's library and applicable from January 1, 2023. This year’s update also includes upstream emissions data and life cycle transmission and distribution losses from the Life Cycle Upstream Emissions Factors 2025, published on October 17, 2025, relating to scope 3 category 3 (Fuel and Energy Related Activities). 

Residual mix factors for European countries are derived from the Association of Issuing Bodies’ (AIB) publication, European Residual Mixes 2024. 

Residual mix factors are not applicable for Austria, Switzerland, and the Netherlands for the 2024 reporting year. 

More detail on some of the methodological changes to Cority's IEA emission factors for electricity is provided below: 

  • Trade adjustment: Electricity values for OECD countries include trade adjustment figures published by the IEA. These adjustments modify the CO₂ component of the electricity emissions factor per kWh, depending on the carbon intensity of imported electricity. IEA does not provide trade adjustment figures for non-OECD countries. 

  • Upstream (Supply Chain) Factors: Cority has included the Total upstream factors from the IEA. For reporting purposes, these are classed under the Supply Chain category in our emission factors. This aligns with our application of upstream factors for DEFRA and other methodologies. Note that the IEA publishes these factors for 150 countries and a global average; therefore, they are not available for some countries and all regional/organizational factors.   

  • Transmission & Distribution Factors: Cority has integrated life cycle adjustment factors for transmission and distribution losses for all countries where these are available. These factors represent emission intensities associated with the transmission and distribution losses of electricity in the grid developed from a life cycle perspective. The total lifecycle T&D loss emission factor is formed by combining the loss associated with the Total upstream factor with the loss associated with the Direct emissions (emissions at the point of generation) for each of the national grids in the dataset. While the IEA presents this as a single aggregated value, Cority has disaggregated it to offer: 

  • T&D loss from the grid (the direct component), listed under the existing Transmission Emissions factor category in Cority and; 

  • A Supply Chain T&D factor (the component associated with the Total upstream factor), listed under the Transmission SC Emissions Factor category in Cority 

For countries, regions, and organizations not included in the Life Cycle Upstream Emission Factors 2025 dataset, the T&D losses adjustment factor from the standard Emission Factors 2025 dataset is used.  

  • Country-specific changes: Emission factor implementation for Albania, Nepal, and Paraguay has been adjusted in the system. These nations do not have data for recent years in the Emission Factors dataset, either due to a lack of coverage or incomplete reporting. For these nations, the most recent year listed is included in Cority's emission factor library (2011 for Albania, 2013 for Nepal, 2015 for Paraguay) rather than listing recent years as zero.
