The new factors are not automatically applied to data that was previously uploaded to the system. Existing data files must be reprocessed to apply the updated emission factors. For support on managing this process or any other questions, please contact Cority Support.

DEFRA 2025 (UK) 

The latest emission factors from Greenhouse gas reporting: conversion factors 2025 published by DEFRA are available with the new factors being applicable from January 1st 2025. No major methodological shifts have taken place, although DEFRA note that air travel and freight values have been revised due to the use of newer flight and aircraft data from the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA), with newer values being lower than in previous years. 

DEFRA Supply Chain Spend – 2025 (UK) 

Supply Chain Spend factors for the UK have been updated. These come from UK and England's carbon footprint to 2022, released in 2025.

While no additional SIC codes have been added, there are significant changes in values from 2021 to 2022. Some of these differences are driven by the move from EXIOBASE v3.8.3 to FIGARO, which increases the UK’s consumption-based emissions. Others are driven by the after-effects of the Covid pandemic, particularly those in the service sector which in some cases have risen significantly between 2021 and 2022. Conversely, the impact per £ spent of some raw materials has reduced significantly as restrictions on production and constraints related to logistics were lifted post-Covid. 

EPA Supply Chain Spend – Addition of NAICS Categories 

The NAICS categories used in v1.2 and v1.3 of the US EPA Supply Chain Factors Dataset have been added to Cority’s emission factor library. The US-Environmentally Extended Input-Output (USEEIO) categories used in versions 1.0 through 1.1.1 and provided for reference in v1.2 and v1.3 are still available in the system but will not be updated beyond the current version.  

The applicable dates for these factors have also been revised to align their applicable dates with the GHG data year. This means that: 

  • v1.1.1 factors (EPA 2022) apply from January 1, 2016 to December 13, 2018, rather than the 2016 calendar year. 

  • v1.2 factors (EPA 2023) apply from January 1, 2019 to December 13, 2021, rather than the 2017 calendar year.

  • v1.3 factors (EPA 2024) apply from January 1, 2022 to Present, rather than January 1, 2018 - Present. 

The above change will not change any existing results for supply chain spend for spend data dated before January 1, 2022 unless data is reprocessed. 

GHG Protocol Cross Sector Tools v2.0 

The methodological update from the Greenhouse Gas Protocol has been added to the system, applying from the start of the release month of March 2024. These are labelled in the system as GHG AR4, GHG AR5, and GHG AR6, based on the GWP values applied. Version 2.0 constitutes a major shift in the categories available for the various types of Mobile Combustion covered by this dataset. From the perspective of Cority's library, that means shifts in the categories and regions available for Transport data source types – Consumption by Fuel, Business Travel, Commuter Travel, and Freight.  

The main change is that the categories have been revised to align directly with the source methodologies of DEFRA and EPA. Previously, categories had been mapped by the GHG Protocol so that the US, UK, and Other regions would have the same categories for mobile combustion.  

For example, in the previous version of Emissions from Cross Sector Tools included three categories for Air Business Travel – Domestic, Short Haul, and Long Haul. These were published for the UK, US and Other regions, with 'Other' being assigned the UK value. In the updated version, US Air Business travel still has three categories – Short Haul (in place of Domestic), Medium Haul, and Long Haul – while the UK has 14 separate values, providing breakdowns by seat class for Short Haul, Long Haul, and International. The 'Other' category is no longer included.  

Overall, the 'Other' region is now largely absent from v2.0. To combat this and reduce calculation errors, we have left v1.0 emission factors that have no equivalent in the new version available in the system with the most recent version of these applying from March 2017. The v1.0 emission factors will be visible in reports such as the Methodologies and Gas Breakdown Report or the data audit dashboard as “GHG Protocol 2017”. Fugitive Emissions Update 

Fugitive emissions have been updated to reflect changes in the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6). This revision covers the existing 235 fugitive gases available within the emission factor library, with all values aligned with either those published by the GHG Protocol or in the case of refrigerant gas blends, calculated using the compositions provided by ASHRAE Standard 24 – 2022 and using the Global Warming Potentials for the constituent gases provided by the IPCC/GHG Protocol.  

Users can select whether to use GHG AR4, GHG AR5 or GHG AR6 to align with their calculation methodology. If DEFRA (UK), EPA (US), NGA (Australia), CGGI (Canada), or OCCC (Spain) are selected for fugitives the system will use the applicable Global Warming Potentials for the AR used for that methodology in that time period. For example, for Defra, AR4 would be used up to the end of 2022, after which AR5 would be used. 

The additional greenhouse gases and refrigerant blends will be added in a future release. These have been held back to allow us to allow us to accommodate the extremely long names of some of the new gases included in the IPCC’s sixth assessment report such as 1,1,2,2,3,3,4,4,4a,5,5,6,6,7,7,8,8,8a-octadecafluoronaphthalene (Perfluorodecalin (trans)). 

CGGI 2025 (Canada) 

Emission factors for Canada have been updated based on the National Inventory Report 1990 – 2023: Greenhouse Gas Sources and Sinks in Canada, published in 2025. These factors apply from January 1st 2023. For this version, we have included estimated Transmission & Distribution losses, relevant for Scope 3, Category 3, based on the 'Unallocated Energy' category published in the National Inventory report. No other major methodological changes have been made by Environment and Climate Change Canada or Cority in our implementation of the emission factors.
