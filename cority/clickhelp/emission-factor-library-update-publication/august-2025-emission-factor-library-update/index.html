The new factors are not automatically applied to data that was previously uploaded to the system. Existing data files must be reprocessed to apply the updated emission factors. For support on managing this process or any other questions, please contact CoritySupport.

EPA 2025 (USA)

EPA emission factors have been updated to reflect the 2025 update. These emissions factors will be applicable from January 1st 2025. This constitutes a change in the date period for this methodology within our library as previous versions up to and including EPA 2024 applied from April 1st. However, we believe this new date is a better reflection of the EPA reporting year as well as aligning with eGrid applicability periods.

Main Changes:

  • Fuel: Gas (Propane) has been added as a category within Fuels.
  • Transport (Consumption by Fuel): Miles per gallon (mpg) assumptions have been updated for all vehicle types. These are used to convert the CH4 and N2O emissions published by the EPA in g/vehicle-mile allowing Cority users to use both distance and fuel consumption for vehicles. For non-gasoline vehicles, Gasoline Gallon Equivalent conversions from the Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) within the US Department of Energy are used.
    • For Passenger cars, mpg values are taken from the 2024 EPA Automotive Trends Report – Greenhouse Gas Emissions, Fuel Economy and Technology since 1975. These range from 13.5 mpg for 1975 to 33.2 mpg for vehicles in 2022. The previous factor used for all periods was 22.5 mpg. For users selecting “unknown” an average of 2007-2022 is used. This value is 28.36 mpg.
    • For Light-duty trucks (vans, pickups, SUVs), the same source is used. Values range from 11.1 mpg for 1975 to 24.2 mpg for 2022. Previously a value of 16.2 mpg was used for all periods. For users selecting “unknown” an average of 2007-2022 is used. This value is 21.07 mpg.
    • For Heavy-Duty vehicles a value of 5.7 mpg is used. For Motorcycles the value is 44 mpg and for Buses the value is 3.4 mpg. These are taken from Average Fuel Economy by Major Vehicle Category published by the AFDC. No differences have been used for different vehicle years. This assumption is supported by On-Road Fuel Economy of Vehicles in the United States 1923-2015 published by the University of Michigan which shows significant mpg improvements for cars and light-duty trucks, but no significant change for medium and heavy-duty trucks.
  • Transport/Equipment (Consumption by Fuel): CH4, N2O emissions have been added for Logging Equipment using LPG fuel using Lawn & Garden Equipment LPG emissions per gallon as a proxy.
  • Road Freight: An updated loading assumption is used for LCVs. This assumes a 57% load factor on a 4000 lbs. total payload and is based on a report from the American Council for an Energy-Efficient Economy (aceee.org). Other vehicle types are unchanged as short ton-mile values are provided directly by EPA.
  • Transport (Consumption by Distance): CO2 emissions for vehicles using biodiesel and ethanol are now classed under the Out-of-Scope category used for biogenic CO2 emissions in the Fuel and Transport (Consumption by Fuel) specifications, thereby aligning the three groups methodologically.

eGrid2023 (EPA 2025) Rev. 2 (USA)

EPA eGrid with 2023 data has been revised. The second revision was released on 12th June 2025 and provides corrections related to net generation values. This version directly replaces the values for EPA 2025 (eGrid 2023) in the existing database which was issued after Revision 1.

CEA 2025 - v20.0 (India)

The source of the emission factor for Indian grid electricity as provided by the Central Energy Authority (CEA) has changed as of the latest publication, with applicable dates from April 1st 2023.

Until version 17 (published October 2021), CEA published a Weighted Average Emission Rate. For versions 18 and 19, CEA published both the original Weighted Average Emission Rate and a new factor for Weighted Average Emission Rate including Renewables. For consistency with previous periods and to take a conservative approach Cority continued to use the Weighted Average Emission Rate for CEA 2023 and CEA 2024. For version 20 (published December 2024) CEA only published the Weighted Average Emission Rate including RES, so Cority will use this figure moving forward. This means there is an 11.7% drop in CO2e per kWh from the previous period.

SEAI 2024 (Ireland)

New electricity and fuel values from the Energy in Ireland report published by the Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland are available. These are based on 2023 emissions data. No significant methodological changes have been noted.
