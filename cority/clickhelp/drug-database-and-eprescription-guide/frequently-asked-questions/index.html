The frequently asked questions are grouped under the following categories:

General — basic ePrescription features and functionality

Interactions and Notifications — information about interactions and the types of notifications supported

Security and Roles — ePrescription security features and roles functionality

Logistics — ePrescription availability and the maintenance of certification

Pharmacy — information about pharmacies and workflows involving pharmacies

ePrescription Layouts and Transactions — information about the eRx Employee Medication layout, its tabs, and ePrescription transactions

The Lexicomp Search — information about the Lexicomp Search and drug database

Reporting and the Transactions Logs — information about custom ePrescription reporting and where ePrescription transactions are logged

 

 

General

 

What type of prescriptions are supported?

ePrescribers can send new employee medication (New Rx) orders, receive refill/renewal requests (Refill) from the pharmacy, and approve or deny those requests.

 

Are all medications available for ePrescribing?

Prescription information for over-the-counter drugs can be entered into Cority, but the drugs cannot be prescribed electronically because they are available without a prescription.

Note: In Lexicomp Search results, over-the-counter drugs are identified as such. Also, drugs identified in the Lexicomp Search results as indeterminate cannot be prescribed electronically either. For more information about the Lexicomp Search feature, see the Lexicomp Search subsection.

 

What are some of the additional benefits ePrescription provides?

With ePrescription, users have access to an extensive drug database for electronic prescribing. For most drugs, the database provides a complete monograph, datasheet, patient information sheet, Rx profile, and accompanying images. The database can be used to perform in-depth research on a drug (including its side effects, its interactions, and any warnings). Each patient information sheet can be printed directly from the Cority application and provided to patients.

 

From where in the application can I send an ePrescription?

Electronic prescriptions can be sent from the Employee Medication eRx layout, which can be accessed in three locations: the Demographics Rx (medication) tab, the Medical Chart Rx tab, and the Clinic Visit Rx tab.

 

Can you edit/change a historical Employee Medication record so that an ePrescription can be sent from it?

If the record is not signed, a user with appropriate permissions can open the corresponding eRx Employee Medication layout, use the Lexicomp Search feature to find the drug in the Lexicomp database, and select the drug from the Search Results popup. That automatically populates the Medication, Medication Strength, and Medication Form fields on the layout. The user can then populate the other required fields such as SIG, Dispense Quantity, Quantity Unit of Measure, Refill Quantity, Pharmacy, and Practitioner (if they are not already populated) and save the record. Once that is completed, the drug can be prescribed electronically.

 

How does adding ePrescription and the Lexicomp Search feature affect use of the existing Medication look-up table?

If an ePrescriber wants to be able to prescribe a drug electronically, he or she must use the Lexicomp Search feature on the eRx Employee Medication layout, instead of the Medication look-up table, to find the drug and create the Employee Medication record. Once that is done, any medical staff who will be changing or updating that employee medication must use the Lexicomp Search to do that. Otherwise, a number of issues (that are described in the answer to the following question) are likely to arise.

 

Do all Occupational Health cloud users need to be granted ePrescription permissions?

If you are adopting the ePrescription functionality, Cority recommends that you grant the default
eRx Prescriber role to all users who access employee medication records—even users who will not be prescribing medications electronically. Cority also recommends that all users with ePrescription permissions use the Lexicomp Search instead of the provided look-up tables to find employee drugs and employee drug allergies, regardless of whether or not the user will be prescribing drugs electronically. If these recommendations are not implemented, the following issues are likely to arise:

  • Drug interactions will be missed. The ability to detect drug interactions is critical to ePrescribing. Drugs obtained from the Medication look-up table and drug allergies obtained from the Allergy look-up table will be not detected by the drug interaction feature, so any related drug interactions will be missed.
  • Non-coded notifications will be generated. For medications obtained from the Medication look-up table, users with access to the Lexicomp database will receive notifications about the medications being non-coded because they do not map to Lexicomp drug codes. The number of these notifications could be excessive and cumbersome to manage, and therefore interfere with the user experience.

Therefore, it is optimal for all users who access employee medication records to use the Lexicomp Search instead of the provided look-up tables for finding drugs and drug allergies. This will allow for more consistent and accurate data.

 

Interactions and Notifications

 

What types of notifications are included with ePrescription?

Users with ePrescription permissions are notified about prescription transactions and any drug-drug, drug-food, drug-allergy, and drug-condition interactions. In addition, Cority notifies these users about employee medications that have not been mapped to a Lexicomp drug code. Those notifications are designated as non-coded.

Note: To be notified of drug-allergy interactions, the drug-allergy for the employee must have been created/reported using the eRx Employee Allergy layout, which provides the Lexicomp Search for drug-allergy selection.


What is a coded vs. non-coded notification?

Any notifications for active employee medications or drug allergies (that is, either they do not have an End Date or they have an End Date in the future) that are not mapped to the Lexicomp database, or employee diagnoses that do not use an ICD-9 or ICD-10 code, are considered to be non-coded. Each non-coded notification advises the user that the employee medication, drug allergy, or diagnosis cannot be included in the interaction detection process because it is not recognized by the Lexicomp database. When an employee medication, drug allergy, or diagnosis is not coded, that increases the likelihood that an interaction will be missed.

Interaction notifications for employee medications recognized by the Lexicomp database are considered to be coded. That is, the corresponding employee medications can be categorized appropriately by the type (drug-drug, drug-food, drug-condition, etc.) and severity of the interaction. Coded interaction notifications for a specific employee medication are displayed on the eRx Interactions tab for that employee medication. Non-coded notifications can be found along with all coded notifications in the eRx Interactions list in the Medical Chart.

 

How are ePrescription users notified about interactions?

If an employee medication has an interaction, an override popup will appear when you attempt to save the Employee Medication record. The override popup displays a message indicating that you must override the interaction, using the Interactions tab, prior to saving the record.

 

Do interactions have different levels of severity?

The severity classification tiers for interactions are Major, Moderate, Minor, Not Applicable (N/A), and Unknown (which is used for notifications for non-coded employee medications and diagnoses). Using the Interaction Severity Level System Override Threshold system setting, you can set the threshold (severity level) at and below which the system will automatically override interactions. Note: Major, the highest interaction severity level, cannot be overridden by the system.

 

If an interaction is overridden, is that action recorded or logged somewhere?

Yes, it is recorded on the eRx Interactions tab for the corresponding patient and employee medication. The notification specifies whether the override was performed by the system or an ePrescriber, the name of the ePrescriber if applicable, the reason for the override, and the date of the override.

 

Are drug warnings included with ePrescription?

Yes, for drugs loaded using the Lexicomp Search on the eRx Employee Medication layout only, warnings will appear on the Rx Datasheet tab.

 

 

Security and Roles

 

What security features limit ePrescription functionality to certain Cority users only?

In Cority, two default ePrescription roles are provided: eRx Admin and eRx Prescriber. With the eRx Admin role, you can perform the administrative tasks associated with ePrescription (such as activating and deactivating ePrescriber service levels). With the eRx Prescriber role you have access to the new drug database, and after you have been through an appropriate identity-proofing process and are registered with Surescripts at the locations from which you will be ePrescribing, you can transmit prescriptions electronically and manage refill/renewal requests. For information on how to register an eRx Prescriber with Surescripts at a health center, see the ePrescription Administrator Workflows section.

For each user who will be granted an ePrescriber role, a separate license must be purchased from Cority. Essentially, you need a separate ePrescription license for every user who is assigned the Lexicomp Security module. ePrescription license usage and capacity is monitored through Cority’s Granted Modules component. Other than notifications about license limits, this monitoring is transparent.

Important: A user should only be provided the ability to prescribe employee medications electronically if he or she successfully meets the legal requirements for prescribing medication (which should be verified by the client and include an identity-proofing process).

 

What tasks can an ePrescriber perform?

An ePrescriber can:

  • perform extensive research on drugs (including their side effects, and any interactions or warnings)
  • prescribe a new employee medication electronically
  • map existing employee medications to Lexicomp for ePrescribing
  • receive refill/renewal requests from the pharmacy
  • view ePrescription statuses and refill/renewal requests
  • manage refill/renewal requests by choosing to approve or deny them, or to prescribe a different employee medication instead
  • view and monitor ePrescription transaction log messages

 

What tasks can an ePrescription administrator perform?

An ePrescription administrator can:

  • grant a user a role (for example, the default eRx Prescriber role)
  • register an ePrescriber at a health center
  • activate an ePrescriber's service levels at a health center
  • deactivate and reactivate an ePrescriber's service levels at a health center
  • unregister an ePrescriber from a health center
  • remove a role from a Cority user
  • view ePrescription administrator (or Surescripts Directory Service) transaction log messages

 

How can I identify a Cority user as an ePrescriber?

On the User layout, ePrescribers will have one or more linked health centers that have a Surescripts Prescriber ID (SPI) number. In addition, each linked health center with an SPI number will have one or more ePrescription service levels. The ePrescription service level (for example: NewRx, Refill, Inactive, etc.) will be displayed next to the linked health center.

Note: An ePrescriber needs to be registered with Surescripts at each health center from which he or she will be ePrescribing. For information on how to do this, see the ePrescription Administrator Workflows section.

 

Can all ePrescribers prescribe controlled substances electronically?

Once a user is able to prescribe medications electronically, there are additional steps for acquiring the ability to ePrescribe controlled substances. For information on setting up a user as an ePrescriber, see the ePrescription Administrator Workflows section. For information on providing an ePrescriber with the ability to ePrescribe controlled substances, see ePrescribing Controlled Substances: Registering with EPCS Gold.


Can you give all providers at a site the ability to prescribe employee medications electronically?

Yes, as long as the client verifies that each provider at the site meets the legal requirements for prescribing medication (which should include an identity-proofing process), then each provider at that health center can be assigned the necessary Cority role.

Note: Any role that provides a user with the ability to prescribe employee medications electronically (for example, the default eRx Prescriber role) must provide the user with access to the Lexicomp module for searching for drugs that can be prescribed electronically. The number of users granted access to the Lexicomp module is limited to the number of eRx licenses the client purchases from Cority.

 

Can an ePrescriber complete ePrescriptions from multiple clinic sites?

Yes, once a user is granted the necessary role, the user can be set up to be able to prescribe electronically from more than one health center. For information on how to do this, see the ePrescription Administrator Workflows section.

 

Can a provider designate a surrogate user to send ePrescriptions or to view or complete refill/renewal requests?

At this time, ePrescription does not support the ability to designate a surrogate or alternate user to access an ePrescriber’s eRx layouts, transactions, or logs, regardless of whether or not the other user has ePrescription privileges. For example, a doctor cannot designate a nurse to send or refill/renew an ePrescription on his or her behalf.

 

What functionality is provided to prevent the same prescription from being sent/used more than once?

If an employee medication has already been prescribed electronically or its record has been printed, the Prescribe Electronically action for its record is disabled.

When an Employee Medication record is printed for the first time or the medication is electronically prescribed, the Ordered By field is populated with the name of the person initiating the action and the Ordered Date field is populated with the current date. If a user prints a record for an employee medication that has already been prescribed electronically, the values in the Ordered By and Ordered Date fields remain the same, and the printed record will have the COPY watermark. If a user prints a record for an employee medication that has already been printed, the values in the Ordered By and Ordered Date fields remain the same, and an incremented print count appears at the bottom.

Also, at present eRx Employee Medication layouts do not provide the Email action.

 

 

Logistics

 

In what countries is the ePrescription functionality supported?

For the following two reasons ePrescription is supported for Cority-hosted, U.S. clients only:

  • To make ePrescription possible, Cority licenses technology from the Surescripts clinical network, which is only available in the U.S.
  • With Cority hosting the system and data, Cority can provide the resources necessary to maintain and support the complex infrastructure required for ePrescription.

 

How often is the Lexicomp database updated, and how quickly is that reflected in the Cority application?

The Lexicomp database is updated approximately once per month. Lexicomp database updates are reflected in the Cority application immediately. The date of the latest update is always displayed at the top right of the eRx Employee Medication layout.

Note: Cority manages the updates transparently. Nothing is required from the client or end user.

 

How often is the Surescripts pharmacy database updated, and how quickly is that reflected in Cority?

The Surescripts pharmacy database is updated daily with a full reload once per week. Surescripts pharmacy database updates are reflected in the Cority application immediately. The date of the latest update is always displayed at the top right of the eRx Employee Medication layout.

Note: Cority manages the updates transparently. Nothing is required from the client or end user.

 

What are the re-certification criteria for maintaining ePrescription? For example, are there annual reviews?

There are no set review times for Surescripts re-certification. Surescripts re-certification is only required if Cority adds new Surescripts functionality or significantly changes the workflow for prescribing an employee medication electronically. Lexicomp does not have a re-certification requirement.

 

Who is responsible for monitoring and alerting about Cority changes or updates that might require Surescripts or Lexicomp re-certification?

It is Cority’s responsibility to maintain Surescripts certification. Lexicomp does not have a re-certification requirement. The Cority Product Team will ensure that the appropriate databases are updated and that the ePrescription process is maintained.

 

How does ePrescription work in terms of Cority upgrades and patches?

ePrescription updates will be provided in much the same way as every other Cority update.

 

 

Pharmacy

 

How can I see which pharmacies support Cority ePrescription?

Cority uses the Surescripts clinical network to connect to pharmacies. The pharmacies connected to Surescripts are listed at http://surescripts.com/network-connections/mns/connected-pharmacies. If you have an NPI, DEA, or SPI number, you can generate an up-to-date report using http://surescripts.com/support/pharmacy-directory.

 

How is the ePrescription pharmacy list determined and set up? Are there any limitations on which pharmacies can be used?

ePrescription obtains its pharmacy list from the Surescripts pharmacy directory. An ePrescriber can access the ePrescription pharmacy list on the eRx Employee Medication layout. Each pharmacy in that list has been certified by Surescripts and is displayed with the services it provides (NewRx, Refill, Change, Cancel, and Controlled Substances).

Note: The pharmacies in the ePrescription pharmacy list are not the same as those listed in the Cority Pharmacy user-defined look-up table. Only ePrescribers have access to the ePrescription pharmacy list.

 

What if the pharmacy to which I want to send an ePrescription is not in the Surescripts pharmacy list?

If the ePrescriber cannot find the desired pharmacy in the Surescripts pharmacy list, then the he or she can print the prescription and forward it to the pharmacy in the appropriate manner.

 

How do I know when a refill/renewal request is sent from a pharmacy?

The Inbox eRx Transactions tab displays all eRx transactions that were initiated by or involve the ePrescriber. Therefore, any refill/renewal request for a prescription initiated by an ePrescriber will be listed on that ePrescriber’s Inbox eRx Transactions tab.

Refill/renewal requests are also displayed on the Employee Medication eRx Transactions tab. This tab displays all completed and pending transactions for the corresponding employee medication.

Note: The Employee Medication eRx Transactions tab only lists transactions specific to the selected employee medication in the corresponding employee’s medical record.

 

How does an ePrescriber handle a refill/renewal request from the pharmacy?

Refill/renewal requests from the pharmacy are listed on the ePrescriber’s Inbox eRx Transactions tab or on the eRx Transactions tab of the specific Employee Medication record. The ePrescriber will select the request and then choose the appropriate action (for example, Refill, Deny, or Deny with new prescription to follow). If the prescriber is approving the request, a popup lets the prescriber either provide the quantity, or if necessary, alter the quantity provided by the pharmacy.

Note: To view pending requests only, the ePrescriber can choose the eRx Pending Prescriber view on the Inbox eRx Transactions tab.

 

For an ePrescription, how do I know what information was sent to the pharmacy?

When an ePrescriber selects the Electronically Prescribe action, an eRx Summary popup appears providing a summary of the ePrescription information, which is to be reviewed and confirmed. The popup resembles a traditional prescription. The information on the popup is what will be sent to the pharmacy if the prescriber clicks Send.

Note: If any required information is missing from the eRx Employee Medication layout, the eRx Summary popup is not displayed and an error message informs the prescriber that required information is missing.

 

How do I know that the ePrescription was sent to the pharmacy successfully?

Once an ePrescriber has selected the Prescribe Electronically action and clicked Send at the bottom of the eRx Summary popup, a message is displayed indicating that either the ePrescription was successfully sent to the pharmacy, the ePrescription is pending, or there is an error. If the message indicates that the ePrescription was successful, the pharmacy’s system acknowledged receipt of it. If the ePrescription is pending, the pharmacy’s system has not acknowledged receipt of the prescription. The pending status will change if the pharmacy acknowledges receipt of the prescription. An error message indicates that there was a problem with the transmission and that the ePrescription was not sent successfully to the pharmacy.


Is it possible to receive a refill/renewal request even though an ePrescription was not sent through Cority?

Yes, for example a pharmacy could generate a refill/renewal request for a prescription that was printed and sent from Cority manually (as opposed to being sent electronically).

Note: There is no guarantee that a pharmacy will enter all of the necessary information correctly when creating a refill/renewal request. ePrescribers are encouraged to review each request, including comparing it to the employee medication record to which it is mapped, to ensure that all of the necessary information is correct.

 

 

ePrescription Layouts and Transactions

 

Once a user is set up as an ePrescriber, are there other configurations or modifications that need to be made within Cority to use ePrescription?

No, the ePrescriber is provided with the new default eRx Medical Chart Split Screen, eRx Employee Allergy, and eRx Employee Medication layouts. The eRx Employee Allergy layout provides the Lexicomp database for drug-allergy selection. The eRx Employee Medication layout provides the Lexicomp database for drug selection and the Surescripts pharmacy directory for pharmacy selection, and enables the ePrescriber to prescribe employee medications electronically.

Important: The inline add/edit feature for employee medications is not available for ePrescribers, because it uses the Medication look-up table associated with the standard (non-eRx) Employee Medication layout. All drug selection for ePrescription must be performed using the Lexicomp Search toward the top of the eRx Employee Medication layout.

 

Can you change the eRx Employee Medication layout?

Changes to the eRx Employee Medication layout are strongly discouraged. In order for an ePrescription to be transmitted successfully, Surescripts requires specific fields on the layout to be populated correctly. If any of the required information is missing or if the required workflow is altered, then the ePrescription functionality may not work. In addition, any significant changes to the ePrescription workflow may require Surescripts re-certification.


What information is required to complete an eRx transaction?

The following fields must be populated on the eRx Employee Medication form for a prescription to be sent electronically:

Medication, Medication Strength, Medication Form, SIG (Instructions), Dispense Quantity, Quantity Unit of Measure, Refill Quantity, Pharmacy, and Practitioner

 

Why is there a Medication Form field and a Quantity Unit of Measure field on the eRx Employee Medication form? Do they not serve the same purpose?

The Medication Form field is required by Surescripts and is automatically populated when the drug is selected from the drug search results popup. The Quantity Unit of Measure field is also required by Surescripts and must be populated for an ePrescription transmission to be completed successfully.

Note: It is possible for the Medication Form and Quantity Unit of Measure values to be different. For example, if the Medication Form field is populated with “tablet” as a result of the Lexicomp Search, an ePrescriber could select a value from the Quantity Unit of Measure picklist that does not match (“solution”, for example). If this discrepancy is not detected by the ePrescriber before the prescription is transmitted, the prescription will be sent without any error message. The ePrescriber is responsible for making sure the Quantity Unit of Measure value is appropriate for the employee medication and Medication Form being prescribed.

 

How is an Employee Medication record signed or locked down? How does this affect ePrescription functionality?

An Employee Medication record is locked as soon as the ePrescription is sent. If the ePrescription is successfully received by the pharmacy, then the Employee Medication record remains locked and becomes signed by the ePrescriber who sent it. If the ePrescriber receives a pending message, the pharmacy has not yet received the ePrescription, and the Employee Medication record remains locked but is not yet signed. If the ePrescription is rejected for any reason, the record becomes unlocked, allowing the ePrescriber to edit the record and make any potential corrections.

 

If an historical Employee Medication record is signed, can an ePrescriber still prescribe it electronically?

If an historical Employee Medication record is signed and an ePrescriber wants to prescribe it electronically, the prescriber must create a new record using the eRx Employee Medication layout and its Lexicomp Search to find the drug.

 

Can an ePrescriber cancel an ePrescription or electronic refill/renewal response through Cority?

An ePrescriber with the Surescripts Cancel service level can cancel an ePrescription or electronic refill/renewal response through Cority.

Note: For employee medications prescribed through pharmacies that do not support electronic cancellation, users will not be able to cancel ePrescriptions or electronic refill/renewal responses through Cority.

 

What is the eRx Transactions tab?

There are two eRx Transactions tabs—one in the ePrescriber’s Inbox and one on each eRx Employee Medication form.

The Inbox eRx Transactions tab lists transactions that were initiated by or involve the ePrescriber. These transactions are not limited to a specific company employee. They include administrative transactions and all Employee Medication record transactions.

The Employee Medication eRx Transactions tab displays all transactions related to the corresponding employee medication and is used to view and manage those transactions.

 

What is the eRx Interactions tab?

This tab lists all interaction notifications for the corresponding employee medication, including notifications for drug-drug interactions, drug-food interactions, drug-allergy interactions, and drug-condition interactions.

 

What is the eRx Datasheet tab?

This tab provides a full reference monograph for the corresponding employee medication. The information is easily navigable using the headings at the left. The monograph provides information about dental dosing, general dosing, drug-food interactions, lactation, pharmacology, as well as side-effect profiles and general warnings.

 

What is the eRx Patient Information tab?

This tab provides patient medication information in an easily digestible format, and the information can be printed from the tab to be given to patients.

 

What is the eRx Rx Profile tab?

This tab provides a hierarchical tree of information for the corresponding employee medication that includes pharmacological classes, allergy classes, indications, and drug-drug and drug-food interactions.

 

What is the eRx Images tab?

This tab displays images (if available) for the corresponding employee medication.

 

 

The Lexicomp Search

 

How do I access the Lexicomp database to complete a drug search?

The eRx Employee Medication layout has the Lexicomp Search feature, which enables a user to search the Lexicomp database for a drug. Simply type the name of the drug (or a part of the name and wildcard characters) and click Search. The search results are displayed in a popup. Select the desired drug from the search results. The Medication, Medication Strength, and Medication Form fields on the layout will then be populated automatically.

 

How do I complete a drug by medical condition search? Are there any limitations to the search?

An ePrescriber can search for drugs by medical condition using the Lexicomp Search on the eRx Employee Medication form. Simply select the Condition radio button under the Lexicomp Search field before performing the search. When you perform the search, the drugs related to the condition will be displayed in the Search Results popup.

 

How do I complete a drug by pharmacological class search?

An ePrescriber can search for drugs by pharmacological class using the Lexicomp Search on the eRx Employee Medication form. Simply select the Pharmacological Class radio button under the Lexicomp Search field before performing the search. When you perform the search, the drugs in the pharmacological class will be displayed in the Search Results popup.

 

What is the purpose of the Search Matched On column in the Lexicomp Search Results popup?

The Search Matched On column lists every instance the search text matched in the Lexicomp database. For example, if the user searches by Drug Name, the column will list every instance of the drug name that it found in the Lexicomp database.

This is particularly important to understand for users searching by Condition or Pharmacological class using wildcards, because the search results can be rather extensive if the search text used is quite general. For example, if you search by Condition looking for drugs related to corneal abrasion and use the search text *abrasion*, the search will list many additional drugs that are not related to corneal abrasion. If you search by Pharmacological Class and use the search text anti*, the search results will list drugs that are antihistamines as well as drugs that are antibiotics. In cases such as the latter, you can use the Search Matched On column to distinguish between the different types of drug.

 

 

Reporting and the Transactions Logs

 

What reporting capabilities are available for ePrescription?

To report on employee medications and prescriptions, an ePrescriber can use the reporting features that are already available in Cority, since eRx employee medication records are stored in the same way other employee medication records are stored.

 

Where are the ePrescription transactions logged and what information is in the log?

All eRx transactions that were initiated by or involve a specific ePrescriber (including administrative transactions) are listed on that ePrescriber’s Inbox eRx Transactions tab. All eRx transactions that involve a particular employee medication are listed on the eRx Transactions tab of the corresponding eRx Employee Medication record. If, for example, an ePrescriber wants to check the status of a pending refill/renewal request transaction, the prescriber could look that up on the eRx Transactions tab of the eRx Employee Medication record.

All ePrescription administrator (or Surescripts Directory Service) transactions are logged in the eRx Admin log, which is located under Administrator > Logs.

 

What are the different views on the Inbox eRx Transactions tab, and what do they provide?

eRx Pending Prescriber – Displays any transaction that is pending action by the ePrescriber.

eRx Pending Pharmacy – Displays any transaction that is pending action by the pharmacy, such as acknowledging the receipt of a transmission.

eRx Errors – Displays any errors that have occurred while the ePrescriber was prescribing employee medications electronically.

eRx Completed – Displays all eRx transactions for the ePrescriber that are completed.

eRx Admin – Displays all administrative transactions pertaining to the ePrescriber. These transactions include activating or deactivating the ability to prescribe electronically, registering or unregistering an ePrescriber with a health center location, and any updating of ePrescriber or health center information.

eRx All – Displays all transactions that were initiated by or involve the ePrescriber.
