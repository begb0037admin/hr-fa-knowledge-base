Terminology and Definitions

 

contraindication – A contraindication is a condition or factor that serves as a reason to reject a particular medical treatment.

controlled substance – A controlled substance is a medication or chemical that is regulated by a government. In the United States of America, such substances are regulated by the Controlled Substances Act. DMT (dimethyltryptamine), Methodone, and Lorazepam are examples of controlled substances in the U.S.

ePrescriber – An ePrescriber is a Cority user who has been granted a role that provides access to the Lexicomp and Surescripts modules (for example, the default eRx Prescriber role), has been through an appropriate identity-proofing process, and is registered with Surescripts at the locations from which he or she will be ePrescribing.

ePrescription administrator – An ePrescription administrator is a Cority user who has been granted a role that enables him or her to perform the administrative tasks associated with ePrescription (for example, the default eRx Admin role).

eRx – Rx is often used to mean prescription. Similarly, eRx means electronic prescription or ePrescription.

identity-proofing – Identity-proofing is the process by which a healthcare practice ensures that the practitioners have the licensure and credentials required to practice medicine and prescribe medication. Cority is not responsible for identity-proofing. It is the responsibility of each healthcare practice.

interaction – An interaction is a situation in which a substance (usually another drug or a food) affects the activity of a drug when both are taken within a certain timeframe.
