System Model Overview

The System Model allows a company or business unit to establish a custom database to track and store its specific compliance information. The database is configured to reflect the company’s regulated facilities, equipment, permits, and conditions. This configuration is visually represented by a System Model that appears in the left pane in the System Model page, in the form of a hierarchical tree.

The following container objects are used when building a System Model tree:

Division

Highest level container, used for organizational purposes only.

Facility/Program

Either a first or second-level object, representing an actual physical facility, a project, or a regulatory program. Defines a single time zone. A parent Division is optional.

Unit/Element

Optional object representing subdivisions of a Facility/Program.

Point of Interest (POI)

Generally used to define a regulatory point of interest, such as equipment, a compliance point or area.

The names of the hierarchical tree components may be changed for any client system upon special request to Cority Customer Support. However, the generic object names are used in all standard documentation.

The compliance components that may be related to the tree objects, and which appear in the right content pane, are:

Requirements

Numeric requirements are used to represent the numerically tracked compliance obligations such as emissions or discharge limits.

Non-numeric requirements are used to represent the tasks, operating procedures, and equipment standards to which a company is subject—such as periodic inspections, sampling procedures, or a requirement for secondary containment.

Event Logs

Manual logs are used to record scheduled activities related to compliance, such as inspection or audit logs, or health and safety incidents.

Automatic logs are used to track deviations whenever a requirement is potentially out of compliance.

Tasks

Tasks may be related to tree objects, Requirements and Event Logs. Any number of these generic components may be used in building the custom System Model.

The components of the System Model may represent actual physical entities (such as a production plant or part thereof) or various regulatory program areas (such as air quality or Title V permits). Most customers actually use a combination of physical locations/equipment orientation and regulatory program areas in configuring their databases (through building their System Models).

This flexibility allows Advanced Calculations (EMS) to be configured quickly to a company’s unique combination of facilities, equipment, and regulatory obligations. Additionally, custom fields can be created, grouped into a Custom Field Template, and applied to System Model objects. These custom fields can be selected for report queries, allowing additional information and tracking options.
