Addressing Elevated Desktop Risk

Understanding Desktop Risk

Desktop Risk is a measure of your risk of developing discomfort based on RSIGuard's objective observations about how you use your computer. Greater exposure (more keyboarding and/or mousing) generally increases your "Desktop Risk", while healthier break taking patterns reduces it. Addressing High Keyboard Usage

RSIGuard measures the amount of time you spend using your keyboard, and how much repetition (keystrokes, and types of keystrokes) you are exposed to. Higher exposure can potentially increase your risk of discomfort, especially if there are any ergonomic issues with your workstation (for example, bent wrists, a keyboard that is to high, or that you have to reach for). Here are some tips to consider if RSIGuard indicates you have high keyboard usage:

  • When you are typing, you should allow your arms to be relaxed at your sides, and your elbows should be relaxed at a 90-110° angle. You may need to raise or lower your keyboard or your seat to accomplish this.
  • Most research indicates that it is beneficial to have your forearms supported while typing – either flat on armrests or on your work surface (do not rest on your elbows). The support should let your arms remain relaxed without pushing your shoulders up. You should make sure no sharp edges push into your wrists or forearms. Your forearms should move left and right easily on the support so you don't end up bending your wrists as you move around the keyboard.
  • If you choose not to use forearm supports and instead float your forearms while typing (for example, if you use a keyboard tray) keep your shoulders and upper arms relaxed. You may want to increase the frequency of breaks you take to help ensure tension is not building up in your upper body.
  • Make sure your keyboard fits you. If you have narrow shoulders, some wide ergonomic keyboards may cause you to have to reach too much. If you have wide shoulders, narrow keyboards (for example, on a laptop computer) may force you into a non-neutral posture.
  • If you find your current keyboard isn't letting you work comfortably, you may consider an ergonomic keyboard that allows you to type in a more neutral posture. If possible, try several different types of keyboards to find a good fit, and allow yourself time to get comfortable with the one you choose. No ergonomic keyboard works for everybody – the best ergonomic keyboard is one that allows you to work more comfortably.
  • Another great way to to address high keyboard usage is to actually reduce your usage. If your job requires you to type a lot of text, you can dramatically reduce keyboard use by using voice recognition software. It may take time to get use to voice typing, but the payoff can be significant.
  • RSIGuard's KeyControl feature can do a lot to help reduce repetitive keyboard (and mouse) activities.
  • You can use RSIGuard's Work Restriction feature to limit your daily exposure to the keyboard. You can also adjust the BreakTimer to be more sensitive to keyboard use so that it suggests more breaks when your typing increases.


Addressing High Mouse (or other Pointing Device) Usage

RSIGuard measures the amount of time you spend using your mouse/pointing device, and how much repetition (clicks, pointer movement, drag-and-drop/selection) you are exposed to. Higher exposure can potentially increase your risk of discomfort, especially if there are any ergonomic issues with your workstation (for example, if you need to reach for your mouse to use it, tense gripping of your device in-between clicks, difficult to push buttons or other issues with your device). Here are some tips to consider if RSIGuard indicates you have high mouse usage:

  • When you are using your pointing device, keep it close to your body so that your shoulder and arm are relaxed. Your elbow should not extend away from your body (either to the side or forward) because doing so keeps your muscles in tension leading to fatigue and potential injury. Use RSIGuard's ForgetMeNots to help you remember to avoid reaching by improving your postural self-awareness.
  • Clicking the mouse repetitively is a common source of discomfort, especially in the hand, wrist, and forearm. If you experience discomfort from clicking, give RSIGuard's AutoClick a try. It takes a little getting used to, but it eliminates most of the need to click, and can be much more effective than switching pointing devices or switching which hand you click with. Click here to view AutoClick documentation, including a video tour of the feature.
  • You can use RSIGuard's KeyControl feature to further reduce the need to click. It lets you create hotkeys to let you double-click, right-click, drag-and-drop, and more without the need to click your mouse. You can also use KeyControl to create hotkeys that automate repetitive tasks, further reducing your mousing (and typing).
  • You can use RSIGuard's Work Restriction feature to limit your daily exposure to your mouse. You can also adjust the BreakTimer to be more sensitive to mouse use so that it suggests more breaks when your mouse usage is higher.


Addressing Low Break Compliance

RSIGuard measures how well you comply with recommended breaks and microbreaks. It also measures your natural breaks (breaks you take without being prompted). Here are some tips to consider if RSIGuard indicates you have low break compliance:

  • The most important way to improve low break compliance is to take recommended breaks.
  • If you find you are frequently skipping breaks, change the Willpower setting in the BreakTimer Interaction Style settings.
  • If you feel break reminders are too intrusive
    • change how breaks are started in the BreakTimer Interaction Style settings,
    • explore the BreakTimer Timing settings to adjust when breaks are suggested,
    • explore the BreakTimer Filters settings to restrict when breaks are suggested,
    • enable the sound warning before breaks so breaks feel less surprising, or
    • pause breaks using the pause button on the main RSIGuard window.
  • If you feel like taking breaks makes you seem unproductive, have a conversation with your supervisor to clarify how your organization feels about you taking recommended breaks.
  • Research shows breaks are a valuable tool for preventing discomfort at the computer. Think about how much you appreciate your health when breaks are suggested!
