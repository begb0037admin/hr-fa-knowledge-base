Hepatitis B Survey (EHS-9629)

If it is clinically necessary, would you like to receive hepatitis B immune globulin? (HEPBGroup1)

New: YES= Efficacy of live attenuated virus vaccines may be impaired by immune globulin administration; revaccination may be necessary. Antibodies in HepaGam B may interfere with some serological tests.

Have you ever been diagnosed with hepatitis B? (HEPB00805)

New: NO= ACIP recommends that adults aged 19–59 years and adults aged ≥60 years with risk factors for hepatitis B should receive HepB vaccines, and that adults aged ≥60 years without known risk factors for hepatitis B may receive HepB vaccines.

If previously vaccinated, please indicate how many doses you received. (HEPB000040)

New: TWO= The 2-dose HepB vaccine (Heplisav-B) was approved by FDA in 2017 and recommended by ACIP in 2018. No difference in occurrence of serious adverse events (GRADE evidence type 1; high certainty evidence) was found for Heplisav-B compared with a 3-dose vaccine (Engerix-B), and serious adverse events were rare for both vaccines.

Are you currently pregnant? (HEPB)

New: YES= Data on Heplisav-B (https://www.fda.gov/media/108745/downloadexternal icon) and PreHevbrio are currently insufficient to inform vaccine-associated risks in pregnancy. Thus, providers should vaccinate pregnant persons needing HepB vaccination with Engerix-B, Recombivax HB, or Twinrix.
